Most are black and white, so colour below comes from the colour photos, the research (straw tops, green bases in mid-April) and DESIGN.md's palette.

## What elephant grass actually looks like

**It is cane, not lawn.** The plants (Saccharum / Imperata / Themeda in the accounts' loose "elephant grass") grow as clumps of stiff, straight, pencil-thin culms, mostly upright, 1.5–2.5 m, and the leaves come off those culms. Photo 03 shows it best: a thicket of near-vertical stems with broad blades leaving them at every height and arching away. 20 shows the same after shelling: the leaves are stripped and only the bare vertical canes are left, like a stand of dead reeds.

**Clumped tussocks.** In the open (01, 04, 09) the stand is a sea of clumps that have grown together. You can still read separate tussocks by their tops: each makes a fountain, a dense vertical core with blades spraying out and over. Between clumps at ground level it is dark.

**Long, broad, arching blades that cross.** Leaves are 1–3 cm wide and up to about a metre long. They rise steeply, then bend over in an arc, often snapping or folding at a sharp crease (06, 05, 08). Blades from neighbouring clumps cross each other in every direction; up close the picture is a lattice of diagonals (03, 08, 11), never parallel verticals.

**Varied heights.** Tops are uneven. Shoulder-to-head height for most (four to six feet), with culms and plumes poking well above at about 7 ft (05, 09, 07). A man is visible from the chest or shoulders up when standing, and vanishes when he crouches (02, 04).

**Seed plumes on some stems.** Silky, feathery panicles on tall culms, pale and bright against the rest (07, 09, 04 top right, 05 skyline). In 09 the whole upper slope glows with them. They lean downwind and catch the light. Not every clump has them; in 07 maybe one stem in three or four.

**Dead straw mixed with live green.** In black and white the stand is mid-grey with lots of pale strokes: old straw blades and culms (pale), live blades (darker), plumes (palest). At the end of the dry season, tops are straw and the green is low down. Dead blades hang, fold, and lie across live ones (06 foreground, 11).

**Trampled lanes.** Where men pushed through (01, 07), the grass is bent over and lies flat along the line of travel, crushed lighter at the top, with darker bare ground showing in the centre. The lane edges are walls of standing grass. Paths are narrow, about one man wide.

**The ground between.** Red-orange laterite, not deep red (12, 17): closer to brick dust (`#a8552f` to `#c07a4a`), with grey stones. Under the grass it is dark litter.

## At three distances

- **6 m (eye level).** Individual blades and stems everywhere; a lattice of crossing diagonals; strong value contrast: sunlit straw edges against a deep dark interior. Grass in the foreground is soft and out of focus, overlapping the man. You see a figure only from the chest up, cut by blades in front of him (02, 04, 10).
- **40 m.** Blades merge into texture. What reads is (1) clumps as soft rounded fountains with bright tops and dark gaps between, (2) a fine streaky vertical texture, (3) pale plumes as specks and wisps above the mass, (4) the uneven top line, and (5) lanes as darker grooves with bent grass along them (01 middle distance, 07 background).
- **150 m.** A continuous pale olive-straw carpet with a soft mottle of darker patches (clump shadow, thinner ground) and a faint vertical grain. Paths and craters read as clear breaks: craters as bare red-brown rings, lanes as thin dark lines. Haze greys it (12 far slope, 09 upper slope).

## What a renderer must get

1. Clump structure: a dark core, blades spraying out and over.
2. Crossing diagonals at 6 m, never parallel verticals (the current spike's "wheat" look).
3. A high-value top and a dark bottom: depth comes from the dark interior.
4. Plumes on a minority of tall stems, palest thing in the frame.
5. Uneven top line; some stems above the mass.
6. Tops straw, bases green, dead blades mixed in.
7. At distance, a carpet with mottle, not a lawn and not a wall of cards.
