# Capt Lawrence "Larry" Himmer, USMC: service history research

Researched 8 Nov 2026. Every claim below carries a source link and page. Anything I could not source is in the "Unconfirmed" list at the end. "PDF p." means the page number in the PDF file; "printed p." means the number printed on the page.

## Bottom line

1. **Mustang: confirmed, by a better source than the Good Conduct Medal.** The Registers of Commissioned and Warrant Officers show prior Marine Corps or Marine Corps Reserve enlisted service, a date of initial entry of **25 Jan 1960**, and a first commission on **29 Jan 1962**.
2. **Good Conduct Medal: not proven.** Its only source is the Virtual Wall icon strip, which uses legacy Coffelt-era medal data. The Chula Vista city biography shows five medals, and the Good Conduct Medal is not among them. See Awards.
3. **"Third tour in Vietnam": no source found.** Nothing I could reach places him in Vietnam before 1968. The family-sheet claim is unsupported, not disproved. The Wall of Faces, Virtual Wall, HonorStates and Find a Grave pages and the Chula Vista biography all give no tour count. The 1/9 chronologies for Jan to May 1968 do not name him.
4. **Commanding C/1/9 through the Khe Sanh siege (Jan to Mar 1968): disproved by the 1/9 command chronologies.** They name other captains as C Company commander for every month from January to April. Himmer is **not named in any 1/9 chronology I read.** The official history shows him commanding C Company on 16 April 1968.
5. **Hills 552 and 689 (7 and 8 April): the records place a different C Company commander there.** The Pegasus roster lists Capt R. H. Flagler for 1 to 15 April. Himmer cannot be placed on those hills.

## Vietnam tours

### Tour count

- **One tour is supported: the one in which he died, with C/1/9.**
- **A second or third tour has no source.** Nothing I found in the 1/9 chronologies, the Registers, the official history or the memorial pages names an earlier tour. Earlier tours with other units are not excluded; I could not search other units' chronologies. The Texas Tech archive search is gated by a CAPTCHA, and I did not attempt to bypass it.
- The one claim of a date for the final tour comes from a search-engine summary. It said HonorStates states he "began his tour on March 27, 1968". **I could not find that text on the HonorStates page itself.** Treat it as unverified. See Unconfirmed.

**Conclusions:**
- **He did not command C/1/9 during the siege.** The chronologies show Hartzell, Harmon, Cargile and Flagler in the job from Jan through Mar.
- **The April and May rosters still print Flagler for the whole month.** Flagler cannot be the commander on 16 April if the official history is right. Either the roster was not updated, or the official history's "Himmer's Company C" refers to a command change the chronology did not record. The chronologies do not settle this.
- **Joining date:** not recorded in any record I read. It was before 16 April, and after the 15 April end date of the Pegasus roster only if that roster is accurate.

**1/9 chronologies read:** Jan, Feb, Mar, Apr and May 1968. The OCR searches (variants of "Himmer") found no hit. OCR is imperfect, so a missed mention is possible. The embedded text layer of the April file is also poor.

### Operations of 1/9 in his period (context; he is placed only in the last)

All dates from the 1/9 April 1968 chronology (1201056016) unless noted.

- **Khe Sanh siege, Rock Quarry, Jan to early Apr 1968.** The battalion held its position northwest of the combat base (Feb chronology, PDF p.4; Defining Year, p.286: "the positions it had defended for 73 days"). Himmer is not on the C Company roster for this period.
- **Operation Pegasus, 1/9 sortie from 4 Apr 1968** (Hill 471). C Company was hit in a pre-dawn assault at 0430. The official history names "Captain Ralph H. Flagler's Company C" (Defining Year, p.286).
- **7 Apr: Hill 552.** D Company and C Company plus the 1/9 command group attacked at 0820 and secured the hill by 0845 (chronology PDF p.8).
- **8 Apr: Hill 689.** C Company led; the hill was secured at 0720. About 38 rounds of 82mm mortar fire followed, with "9 KIA's, 27 serious WIA's" for the day (chronology PDF p.8).
- **15 Apr: Pegasus ended and Operation Scotland II began** (chronology PDF p.5 and p.52; Defining Year, p.312 footnote).
- **16 Apr: the fight southwest of Hill 689** (chronology PDF pp.10 to 11). The chronology says 1/9 "deployed 'C' Company (-) and 'D' Company (-) at approximately 14[3]5" (the OCR reads "1h35"). It says C Company "positioned itself to the South of the ridge", "with one platoon on line and another in trace". The assault stopped after about 300 meters, and the units returned to the Hill 689 perimeter at 0230 on 17 April. The Defining Year (p.314) says two platoons of C Company moved out "at noon" under Cahill and were ordered to attack from the south. The two sources differ on the hour.

His part on Hills 552 and 689 on 7 and 8 April: **not supported.** By the Pegasus roster, Flagler commanded C Company then.

3. **Commissioning route** (OCS, PLC, ECP, or another). Not in any record I reached. Also unknown: his enlisted rank, enlisted units, and how the Jan 1962 commission relates to his SDSU graduation.

8. **"Bronze Star" and "Vietnam Gallantry Cross."** Single-source (Chula Vista biography), no citation.
9. **Wall of Faces pages 2 and 3** (the Sharon Himmer remembrance and others): blocked by a Cloudflare check.

## How the family can get the rest

1. **Official Military Personnel File (OMPF).** Ask the National Personnel Records Center (NPRC), St. Louis. Next of kin of a deceased veteran can request it with an SF-180 or the online eVetRecs process; the request must be signed and dated by next of kin and include proof of death (a death certificate, funeral-home letter or published obituary). The NPRC mailing address is 1 Archives Drive, St. Louis, MO 63138. Source: <https://www.archives.gov/veterans/military-service-records>.
   - Records open to the public 62 years after separation (same page). He separated in 1968, so the file is not open to the public until 2030, and next-of-kin status matters until then.
   - Ask for the **full OMPF** and for the **Basic School and officer fitness reports**, not only the DD Form 214. Give the service number, date and place of birth, and date of death. Add "the 1973 fire" fields if the clerk asks.
   - **What it should contain:** enlistment papers, enlisted rank and unit history, the route to commission (OCS, PLC, ECP or other), schools, fitness reports by unit and year, overseas orders, medals and citations (including the Bronze Star and any Good Conduct Medal), and the casualty and burial paperwork. I have not seen a file, so this is a statement of what the NPRC page and the Register's data imply, not a catalogue.
2. **The 1973 fire.** The NPRC says the fire destroyed Army records for personnel discharged 1 Nov 1912 to 1 Jan 1960 and Air Force records for personnel discharged 25 Sep 1947 to 1 Jan 1964 (names after Hubbard, James E.). The NPRC lists no Marine Corps loss. Source: <https://www.archives.gov/personnel-records-center/fire-1973>.
3. **Replacement medals and awards:** the NPRC page above handles them. A citation for the Bronze Star would settle whether it is real and whether it was for valor.
4. **USMC History Division, Quantico.** Ask for any command chronology and the Hill 689 / Scotland II casualty investigation (the Cuenin report mentioned in Defining Year, p.316). Ask it also for the 9th Marines regiment chronology for April 1968.
5. **Texas Tech Vietnam Center and Sam Johnson Vietnam Archive.** A person can solve the CAPTCHA that blocks the archive search, then search "Himmer" and read the 9th Marines and earlier unit chronologies. That is the quickest way to test the "third tour" claim.
6. **Fold3, Ancestry and muster rolls.** I did not sign in. Marine muster rolls (monthly unit rosters) for 1960 to 1962 should show his enlisted unit and rank. The family can search them with an account.
7. **Veterans.** Jim Thompson (C/1/9, Wall of Faces, 2020) and the 1/9 association can speak to the company. Hubert Hunnicutt is the Marine who stayed with the company commander on 16 to 17 April; his Navy Cross citation does not name Himmer.
