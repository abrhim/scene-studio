# Capt Lawrence Himmer, 16-17 April 1968: research

Written 2026-11-08 by the scene-researcher. Every claim carries a reference and a layer. Scratch copies of what I read are in `scenes/himmer-1968/.scratch/research/` (chronology PDF and page images, Defining Year text, article text).

**Layer key.** `text` = a primary or official text says it. `official history` = Shulimson et al., *The Defining Year* (written from the 1968 investigation, the chronologies and later statements). `veteran account` = a participant's words, or a magazine article built on them. `secondary` = a memorial or student page. `calc` = my own calculation from the DEM or from astronomy (not a source). `silent` = the sources say nothing.

**Abbreviations.**
- **ComdC** = 1st Bn 9th Marines Command Chronology, April 1968.
- **AAR** = the Pegasus After Action Report bound into the ComdC.
- **DY** = *The Defining Year*.
- **HN** = McGuire, "Rescuing the Walking Dead" (HistoryNet / *Vietnam*, Feb 2020).
- **NC** = Navy Cross citation for Hubert H. Hunnicutt III.
- **Sheet** = `sources/himmer-memorial-sheet.html`.

## 0. What the research changes (read this first)

1. **Himmer did not die leading the attack, on the evidence.** The official history says Company C advanced up the ridge from the south and that "Himmer, both platoon commanders, a platoon sergeant, and several squad leaders fell with wounds." He was wounded, and he was alive overnight. Cpl Hunnicutt's Navy Cross citation has him finding the company commander "lying in an open area, severely wounded" at dusk. Hunnicutt moved him to cover at first light on 17 April. The official history says Hunnicutt claimed Himmer was alive "as late as the afternoon of the 17th," and "Himmer was never seen alive again." No source I could read says when, where or how he died. The Wall date of 16 April is a record convention.
2. **The Command Chronology does not confirm Himmer as CO of Company C.** Its roster lists Capt R. H. Flagler as Company C commander for 1-30 April. The AAR task organisation gives Flagler 1-15 April. Himmer is not named anywhere in the 62-page chronology that I could read (I checked by eye, because the OCR is poor). The official history and the Navy Cross citation do call him the C Company commander. When he took command is **silent**.
3. **The enemy unit is disputed.** HN names the 66th Regt, 304th Div. The ComdC AAR names the 2d Bn, 101st Regt, "dug in a reverse slope defense."
4. **The Army sighting rests on one source, HN.** HN gives no time and no recipient. The "morning" in the Sheet is not in HN. No Marine record I read mentions the sighting.
5. **The ComdC's account of Company D is contradicted by the official history.** The ComdC says Delta "swept up and over the ridge." DY footnote 14 says that cannot be supported by any participant's statement. Do not show Delta overrunning the bunkers.
6. **Hill 552.** The 1/9 Hill 552 is at **XD 823 403** (ComdC). The 552 you saw near Co Put is printed **527** on the sheet.
7. **Times of day.** The time zone of the logs is not stated in what I read. This shifts sunset by an hour (section 8).

**Why there were delays (text).** Meyers: "Because of the necessity of the various radio relays, Meyers did not receive this message until 1543" (DY fn p. 314, official history's paraphrase). Tompkins found "inexcusable" failures in reporting to division (DY p. 316).

### The Army air-cavalry sighting that never reached the Marines
- **Source:** HN only. Air-cavalry scouts "radioed an Army unit on a nearby hill" on 16 April, reporting that all elements of the 66th Regt, 304th Div had moved back near Highway 9 and Lang Vei. HN says this "was not passed along to Marine commanders."
- **Time of day, caller, receiver and callsigns: silent.** The Sheet's "morning" and "within Army channels" are not in HN.
- **No Marine record I read mentions it** (ComdC, DY).
- **Corroboration:** none found. DY (p. 313) places the 2d Brigade, 1st Cav, in Task Force Glick, operating east of Khe Sanh.
- **For the scene:** show it as a message that leaves one radio and never arrives, labelled as HN's claim (veteran account/magazine), with no clock time.

### Means named in the sources
Radios are named generically ("radio," "relays," "monitoring radio traffic"). **No source I read names the PRC-25** or any Marine set. Do not label one by model. The ComdC names a captured Chinese **PRC-10** radio (Pegasus AAR, 4-5 April; ComdC p. 53). It is the enemy's.

### Hill 552 and the map
- **ComdC (text):** Hill 471 = XD 847 394; Hill 552 = **XD 823 403**; Hill 689 = XD 803 409 (ComdC p. 3). DY p. 287 puts "the ridge between Hill 552 and Hill 689" two km north of Route 9.
- **DEM check (calc):** at XD 823 403 the DEM has a **552 m** top within about 240 m (px 377,286). That agrees with the ComdC.
- **On the 1968 sheet**, a spot height reads "…52" at about XD 823 403, with its first digit hidden under a track. I read it as 552, not certain.
- **The Co Put peak** (XD 79 38) is printed **527**, not 552. The terrain note's "552 near Co Put" is a misreading. Hill 552 is **east** of Hill 689, between 689 and Khe Sanh.

### Does the 610 m summit match "Hill 600"?
**The sources do not give a grid for the battle hill, so nothing proves it.** What agrees:
- DEM summit 610 m at 16.6368 N 106.6789 E, 1.42 km from the 689 summit at bearing 217° (calc). HN says "about a mile southwest."
- Straight-line distance from the Khe Sanh base grid (XD 841 421) to that summit is about 5.1 km (3.2 miles). HN says "about 3 miles west."
- The summit is 610 m (about 2,000 ft), and HN says "nearly 2,000-foot height."
- On the 1968 sheet a 6084 route shield and a track run along this ridge. No printed spot height is there, and the 600 contour label I saw lies on 689's own southeast flank.

What disagrees or is open:
- DY's "1,000 meters" from the landing point back to 689 suggests the fight was nearer than 1.42 km. A Marine perimeter around 689 would account for some of that.
- The A Co patrol went out "southwest of the battalion's perimeter on Hill 689 and a small adjoining hill" (DY). The ComdC says the A Co contact area of 13 April was "south of 1/9 position" and "along Dong Dang Ridge."
- "1,800 of 2,000 feet" (HN) is an elevation reading, since the profile below shows only a gain of about 80 m from the saddle. About 50 yards below the summit, the DEM gives 584-610 m (1,916-2,000 ft); 1,800 ft = 549 m lies roughly 150-250 m from the summit on most sides. The loose figures are consistent with the DEM.

**Profile 689 to the 610 m summit (calc):** 691 m at the start, 588-596 m at 380 m out, 466 m at the **saddle, 950 m out**, then up to 610 m at 1,424 m. These are modern ground heights, not 1968.

## 8. Weather and light

**No source I read states the weather on 16 April 1968.**

- Evidence for clear enough to see and fly:
  - the whole day's action was fought in view of the ridge; Banks and Cahill saw it;
  - Army gunships and an air observer worked on 17-18 April (ComdC p. 12; HN);
  - a Cessna AO saw a man "waving his foot" from a crater at midday on the 17th;
  - the first medevacs arrived about 1805.
- **Haze and fog:** the AAR (p. 60) says movement on 4 April was helped by "limited visibility," and "once the ground haze/fog lifted" fire came down. That is 4-5 April, not 16 April. DY describes Khe Sanh mornings of fog and low cloud in January and February (DY p. 483, "cracbin"). A morning fog on 16 April is **plausible** but **silent**.
- **Rain:** none reported.
- **Heat:** HN says sweat "streamed" down Hatcher's face, and that the work in flak jackets was hot.
- **Twilight and night:** HN says the fight "dragged on into twilight." Hunnicutt left his crater "at dusk" (NC). The withdrawal began at 2115 and ended at 0230-0310.

**The time zone is not stated in what I read.** The chronology uses an "H" suffix. If H means UTC+8 (the usual US military zone letter; this is from my memory and **unverified**), the table is on the left; if the logs are local solar-ish UTC+7, on the right.

## 10. Graphic content in the accounts (flagged for the "no blood" treatment)

Each item says what the sources describe, not how to show it.

1. **Head shots.** "Four Marines with single shots to the head" (DY); Panykaninec "hit in the head" (HN). Show as a man falling.
2. **Mutilation.** The ComdC (p. 12) and DY (p. 315) describe dead Marines on the ridge as decapitated and disembowelled. I recommend no depiction, and no label.
3. **A wounded man left alive.** Himmer, severely wounded, alone overnight with one corporal, then "never seen alive again." Hunnicutt thinking "I'm walking dead" (HN). Panykaninec waving a foot from a crater, believed a decoy. These are the hardest ideas, but they can be shown without injury detail.
4. **Dead left behind.** The order to leave the dead. 20 killed whose bodies were not recovered.
5. **Helicopter incident.** A tail ramp crushing an NVA soldier; four NVA shot at 10 m. Show as a flash and a fall, if at all.
6. **Enemy dead:** the NVA were mostly unseen. Do not add them.
7. **Friendly fire risk:** an artillery mission landing within 50 m of C Co.
8. **The Himmer family** is a living audience. Say plainly that his death's time and place are not in the records.
